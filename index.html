Battle of the Atlantic: Destroyer and U-boat

A strategy game played by ear, set in the Battle of the Atlantic, 1939 to 1945. Hunt U-boats with a Royal Navy destroyer's ASDIC, or command a German U-boat against a convoy. All convoys, ships and people are invented. It's tense cat and mouse, not a horror game.

Start here

Sound only starts when you tap a button. On an iPhone, if you hear nothing, check the ring and silent switch and turn the volume up.

    How to play

    Unlocking the U-boat

    You start on the destroyer. Pass level 1 on the destroyer side to unlock the U-boat side. On each side, pass a level to open the next one. Training missions are always open, and don't count as level 1. Your progress is saved on this phone.

    Playing the destroyer

    1. Your ship starts patrolling ahead of the convoy at 15 knots. The ASDIC sweeps from side to side, pinging every few seconds.
    2. When you hear an echo come back after a ping, the operator calls "Contact" with a bearing: green is to starboard, your right; red is to port, your left. "Green four five" is 45 degrees to the right.
    3. Tap Steer for the contact. The helmsman aims ahead of her, using the plot.
    4. Listen as you close. The gap between ping and echo shrinks. A higher echo than the hiss after the ping means she's coming towards you (high Doppler); lower means she's moving away. "Moving left" or "moving right" tells you which way she's crossing.
    5. Stay at 15 knots or below. Above 18 knots the ASDIC is deaf, and the U-boat hears your screws speed up and may turn hard.
    6. Close in, the beam passes over her and you lose contact. The range when that happens tells you her depth: in feet, about three quarters of the range in yards. Lost at 300 yards means about 225 feet. On Easy, the First Lieutenant sets the depth; on Normal he suggests it; on Hard it's up to you.
    7. Keep straight on. On Easy and Normal a bell rings and he says "Fire". Tap the big Fire depth charges button. The charges take a while to sink, so the explosions come later.
    8. Turn right round and find her again. A hit brings up oil and wreckage, or forces her to the surface.
    9. From level 3 you also have the Hedgehog. It throws 24 small bombs 250 yards ahead, so you fire while you still hold contact, with her right ahead at about 250 yards. They only explode if they hit.
    10. Watch out for fish, which give woolly echoes, and from level 4 for bubble decoys: firm at first, but they never move.

    Playing the U-boat

    1. Dived, you hear ships through the hull. Steamers have slow, heavy, rhythmic screws. Escorts have fast turbine screws, and they speed up when an escort starts an attack run.
    2. Ask the sound room for bearings. Bearings run clockwise from your bow: zero four five is ahead to the right, two seven zero is to your left.
    3. At periscope depth, 14 metres, take short looks through the periscope. Each look improves the firing solution. Go slowly: at speed the periscope throws up spray that escorts can see.
    4. Tap Steer to attack the target. Fire from about 1,000 to 2,500 metres, with the target side-on to you. Nearly bows-on or stern-on is a narrow target. Tap the big Fire torpedo button, then listen to the torpedo run.
    5. Then get deep and quiet: choose a depth, turn on silent running and turn away from the escort. When the sound room says the escort is starting his attack run, turn hard and go deeper. His charges are set for where he thinks you are.
    6. Watch the battery and the air. Full speed dived drains the battery fast. On the surface you're fast, 17 knots, and you recharge, but escorts can see you, and at night radar can find you.
    7. From level 3 there's a cold water layer at about 80 metres that weakens the escorts' ASDIC, and you carry Pillenwerfer decoys.

    Saving

    Tap Save this mission and leave at any time, and resume it later from Choose a mission. Long missions, marked as long, also save themselves every minute. There's one save slot, so a new saved mission replaces the old one.

    Learn the sounds

    Glossary

    Royal Navy

    • ASDIC: the British sonar. It sends a ping and listens for the echo.
    • Green and red: starboard (right) and port (left). "Green four five" is 45 degrees to the right of the bow.
    • High Doppler, low Doppler: the echo is higher, or lower, in pitch than the hiss after the ping. High means the target is coming towards you.
    • Number One, or the First Lieutenant: the captain's second in command.
    • Pattern: a set of depth charges dropped from the rails at the stern and fired from throwers at the sides.
    • Hedgehog: an ahead-throwing weapon of 24 small bombs that explode only on contact.
    • HE, hydrophone effect: propeller noise heard on the ASDIC set.
    • Action stations: everyone to their fighting positions, sounded on the alarm rattlers.

    Kriegsmarine (German navy)

    • Herr Kaleun: how the crew address the captain, short for Herr Kapitänleutnant.
    • Alarm!: the order to crash dive.
    • Fluten: flood the tanks, to dive. Anblasen: blow the tanks, to surface.
    • Horchraum: the sound room, where the hydrophone operator, the Horcher, listens.
    • Schraubengeräusche: propeller noises.
    • Zerstörer läuft an: the destroyer is starting his attack run.
    • Wasserbomben, or Wabos: depth charges.
    • Schleichfahrt: silent running.
    • Rohr eins, los!: tube one, fire! Fächer: a fan, a spread of torpedoes.
    • Treffer: a hit. Fehlzünder: a dud.
    • LI: the chief engineer, who keeps the boat at the right depth.
    • Pillenwerfer: a bubble decoy that gives an ASDIC echo like a U-boat.
    • Kriegstagebuch: the war diary, the U-boat's log.

    History notes

    • Destroyers made about 30 knots, but their ASDIC was swamped by their own noise above about 18 knots.
    • The ASDIC beam couldn't look steeply down, so contact was lost as the ship closed. The deeper the U-boat, the further out it was lost.
    • A Type VII U-boat made about 17 knots on the surface and 7.6 knots dived. It was normally safe to about 220 metres, and its battery lasted only an hour or two at full speed dived.
    • A U-boat at periscope depth was 13 to 14 metres down. A crash dive took around 30 seconds.
    • Hedgehog came into service in 1942, and the Pillenwerfer decoy about the same time.
